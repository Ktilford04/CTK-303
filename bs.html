Scale of C Major

Basic Chords

Major

Major triads are constructed by playing the first, third, and fifth degrees of the scale associated with the chord. For example, C major would be made up of C, E, and G because those are the I, iii, and V of the C major scale. This applies to any chord, the scale used will be derived from the root note of the chord.

Minor

Minor chords are constructed similar to major chords, except that the third degree is flattened a half step. So for C minor, we would still use the C major scale to construct, except we flatten the third, leaving us with C, Eb, and G.

Suspended

Suspended chords often seem confusing but they are actually very simple. There are two types of sus chords, sus2 and sus4. The reason they are called suspended chords is because the third of the chord is removed, or in other words, suspended. In place of it, the second or fourth degree of the scale is used, creating a different effect. This is how we get the names sus2 and sus4 respectively. Therefore Csus2 would be made up of C, D, and G for example.

Augmented

Instead of altering the third, augmented chords are made by raising the fifth of the chord a half step. In our example of C, this would be C, E, and G#. Augmented chords are typically only built off of major chords. It is possible to raise the fifth and lower the third, creating a minor augmented chord, but when you raise the fifth AND lower the third, you essentially create a different major chord. For example CmAug is the same set of notes as an Ab major chord in first inversion, and would more commonly be referred to as that.

Diminished

Diminished chords are similar to augmented, except that instead of raising the fifth a half step, you lower it. Diminished chords can also only be created based off a minor chord, or in other words, with the third flattened as well. If you flatten the fifth on a C major chord, it would be referred to as C(b5), not diminished. Cdim would be constructed of C, Eb, and Gb.

7th Chords

Dominant 7th

Dominant 7ths are the most common type of 7th chord, and is what is being referred to if a chord is simply notated with a 7 after the chord name. Dominant 7ths are created by adding a flattened version of the 7th degree of the major scale, or the minor 7th on top of the triad you started with. Dominant sevenths can be and commonly are added to both major and minor triads. A C7 chord would consist of C, E, G, and Bb.

Major 7th

Major 7th chords are very similar to dominant 7ths, except that instead of adding a minor 7th, you just add the 7th degree of the major scale associated with the chord. For C this would be C, E, G, and B. This can be applied to both major and minor triads, and would be notated as is written as Cmaj7 and Cm(maj7), respectively.

Diminished 7th

Diminished 7th chords, as you may guess, are constructed starting with a diminished triad, but with a double flat 7th degree added (lower the 7th degree a whole step). In reference to the major scale, this would make the formula for a dim7 chord I, biii, bV, bbvii. For our example of Cdim7, this would beC, Eb, Gb, and A.

Half Dim / m7(b5)

Half dimimished chords are essentially the exact same as dim 7th chords, except that the seventh degree is only lowered a half step as opposed to being double flat. This can also be thought of as a minor dominant 7th chord but with a flat fifth. This is often also notated as m7(b5). The notes present in Cm7(b5) are C, Eb, Gb, and Bb.

Extentions and Add Chords

9ths, 11ths, 13ths

Extended chords (9ths, 11ths, 13ths, etc) are similar to 7th chords but just with stacking more notes that are in the scale. a 9th chord has the tonic, the third, the fifth, the dominant 7th, and the 9th (the 2nd but an octave higher) present in it. The same is true for the 11th, but with the 11th added on top of that. For example C11 would have C, E, G, Bb, D, and F. Extended chords always default to using the dominant 7th, not the major, unless specified; such as Cmaj11

Add Chords

Add chords are exactly as they sound. You start with the standard triad, and then instead of stacking up the degree specified, you just add whatever scale degree is notated. For example, Cadd9 has C, E, G, and then D an octave higher since that is the 9th.
